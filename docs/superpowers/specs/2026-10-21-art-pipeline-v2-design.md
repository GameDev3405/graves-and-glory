# Art pipeline v2: Blockbench models, the style guide and LabPBR maps

Approved by Kort, 2026-10-21 (questions round 25). Builds on the Task 5 pipeline (`tools/build_skins.py`, `TroopModelLoader`)
and the troop depth design (`2026-10-21-troop-depth-design.md`, "depth N" below means its section N). The spike that
backs it is `bannerbound-p2b/work/p2b/spike-bb/` (side-by-side.png, the two `.bbmodel` files, `convert.py`), reported
in `.superpowers/sdd/2026-10-17-phase-2-houses/art-v2-spike-report.md`. Nothing here is in the mod yet.
Kort ruled on K1 to K3 (section 10) on 2026-10-21 (questions round 25) and the rulings are marked where they bite. The Controller
has ruled on the rest; each ruling is marked "(Controller's call)".

## 1. Intent

Kort (2026-10-21) wants the troops and weapons to look better than they do, in the way the Soulrend modpack's weapons
do: hand-shaped, chunky, 3D, and made entirely in-house. Three directions are approved: Blockbench as the workshop, a
Minecraft 3D texture rulebook in place of the top-down pixel-art skill, and LabPBR `_n`/`_s` maps for the Complementary
shader.

**Goals**
- Weapons are hand-shaped cube models (`.bbmodel`), painted by the style guide, converted by our build tool, drawn by
  our own renderer. A sword has a ridge and a flared guard; a bow is a recurve whose limbs flex through the pull frames.
- Troop rigs and skins can be opened, edited and painted in Blockbench by Kort or by an agent through the MCP plugin,
  from the same source files the build reads.
- One texture rulebook (`docs/ART-STYLE-GUIDE.md`) for troops, relief and weapons: ramps, light, face brightness,
  material patterns.
- `_n` and `_s` maps ship for every troop sheet and weapon sheet, generated by the build from a material map, and the
  build checks them.
- Everything stays inside the Task 5d frame budget (depth 5) and the 128-quad weapon cap.

**Non-goals**
- No change to the rigs' bone names, the 18 clips, the hand bones or the grip rule (the grip pixel's centre is the
  weapon's origin on the hand pivot).
- No change to how team cloth is drawn (one sheet per side, team parts), to the glow pass, or to the relief layer.
- No meshes (Blockbench's free-form meshes), no per-vertex animation, no Blockbench animations: our clips drive bones.
- No new texture sizes for troops: 64 x 64 (128 wide for the colossus and large beasts) stays.

**Anti-goals**
- Anything that needs an outside mod: no 3D Skin Layers, no EMF/ETF, no CEM, no GeckoLib. Kort's ruling stands.
- Copying any Soulrend model or texture. The research measured their files; nothing is reused.
- Hand-painting `_n`/`_s`. Painters paint colour and a flat material map; the build makes the PBR maps.

## 2. Source format

### 2.1 Weapons: `art/weapons/<id>.bbmodel`

One Blockbench project per weapon, **Generic Model** format (`free`), 1 unit = 1 model px, y up, the grip pixel's centre
at the origin, the big faces across z. The rule set the converter enforces:
- Top-level groups are the frames: `rest`, and `pull_0`, `pull_1`, `pull_2` for a draw weapon. Nothing sits at the root.
  Every frame is complete on its own (the bow's grip is in every frame; Blockbench's duplicate keeps the UVs).
  Every weapon has a `rest` group. No hand-made silhouette cube is needed: the converter builds the weapon's far
  version from the `rest` group (section 3, section 8) (Controller's call).
- Cubes only. Rotation on any axis, about the cube's own origin. Groups may carry pivots and rotations; the converter
  composes them.
- One colour texture, `<id>.png`, 32 x 64, linked by relative path and embedded; UV size equals the bitmap size, so a UV
  unit is a texel. Every face has its own patch, 1 texel per model px (`sheet.face` in the spike's `paint.py` is the
  allocator an agent uses; Kort paints in Blockbench's paint mode on the same patches).
- Beside it: `<id>_m.png` (the material map, section 6), and for a lit weapon `<id>_e.png`, both the sheet's size.
- The project's `name` is the weapon id. The texture's `uv_width`/`uv_height` are the sheet size.
- Grip and pull data move out of the model into `art/weapons/<id>.json`, which keeps only `weapon`, `thickness`
  (informational, checked against the model's z extent), `grip {offset, rotation, note}`, `pull` and `nocks` (now in
  model px of the weapon frame, since there is no sprite pixel to point at), `note`. `pixel` goes: the origin is the grip.

**Generated:** `models/troop/weapon/<id>.json` at **format 3**:
- the keys of format 2, with `size` the sheet size;
- `frames[]` of `{name, nock, quads}` where a quad is **20 numbers**: the four corners in model px, counter-clockwise
  seen from the side the normal points to, then the texels of the four corners. The normal is the corner cross product,
  normalised at load. An axis-aligned quad is the same thing with its corners spelled out, so the relief layer's
  13-number quads and the weapon's 20-number quads both load through `TroopMesh.parse`, which tells them apart by
  length;
- `texture`, `normal`, `specular`, `glow`: the colour sheet and its `_n`, `_s`, `_e` ids (`normal` and `specular` are
  informational for the loader; Iris finds them by suffix, section 6).

The weapon frame is the sprite frame of depth 2 (x right, y down, z thickness): the converter turns Blockbench's y-up
space by a half turn about x (`(x, y, z) -> (x, -y, -z)`), a proper rotation, so winding is kept and north becomes south.
The spike's `convert.py` is the proof: 66 quads for the sword, 72 to 78 for the bow frames, winding checked against the
13-number form for every unrotated cube (60 of 60 agree).

### 2.2 Troops: `art/troops/<troop>.bbmodel`

One project per troop, the same format, with:
- a group per rig bone, named as the rig names it (`body`, `head`, `right_arm`, ... `right_hand`), with
  pivots in the parent's frame. The cubes and pivots in a troop's bone groups are authoritative. The rig JSON stays the
  skeleton of record for bone names, parents, rest rotation, clips, hands and `outer`: the converter checks the groups
  against it and fails on a missing or extra bone, a wrong parent, or a rest rotation that differs from the rig's. Rest
  rotation is taken from the rig, so the biped's `arms_folded` at -43 degrees is not an error. Where a troop's pivot or
  box differs from the rig, the converter writes a per-troop override, exactly as the current JSON does, so `mage` and
  `gl_marshal` reach the byte-identical gate (Controller's call);
- box-UV cubes in those groups for the skin boxes (`uv` is the box's sheet offset, `mirror` as today), with the troop's
  per-bone `scale` and `hunch` still in `<troop>.json` and baked by the build (Blockbench shows the unscaled rig; the
  build's bake is unchanged);
- parts: a group named `part:<part>[:<name>]` for each entry of today's `parts[]`, whose cubes are the part's boxes
  placed at the part's pivot. The part library `art/parts/<part>.json` stays the source of a part's boxes; the
  converter checks the group's cubes against it (so a part is still the same part on every troop) and takes from the
  `.bbmodel` only what today's entry holds: `uv`, `offset`, `rotation`, `scale`, `stages` (the entry keys live in the
  group's name suffix and in `<troop>.json`'s `parts[]`, which stays as the place for `stages` and `scale`);
- textures: the stage skins `<troop>_s1.png` to `_s3.png`, `_veteran`, `_e`, `_o` and the relief layers `_s1_r.png` ...
  linked by relative path. The build reads the PNG files on disk, never the embedded copies, and `--verify` fails when
  an embedded copy differs from the file (someone painted in Blockbench and forgot to save the texture);
- team parts: a group `team:<part>` whose cubes UV onto a second texture, `art/team/team_cloth.png` (the greyscale
  template). The converter takes the UVs; the build still makes `team_cloth_<side>.png` per side and the tint rule still
  forbids team hues on a skin.

The troop `.json` keeps `troop`, `rig`, `size`, `bones` (scale only), `hunch`, `idleArms`, `parts[]` (`stages`,
`scale`), `skins`, `veteran`, `glow`, `glowFrom`, `outer`, `weapon`, `hand`, `portrait`. The geometry keys (`pivot`,
`boxes`, `uv`, `offset`, `rotation` of parts) move into the `.bbmodel`.

**Carry-over, item by item:**
- *Rig bones:* groups named by bone; the rig JSON is the skeleton of record. `bake_bones` is unchanged; it reads bones
  from the converter instead of `<troop>.json`.
- *Stage skins:* the same PNG files, now also listed as Blockbench textures so Kort sees each stage on the model (switch
  texture in the Textures panel).
- *Team cloth tint:* unchanged; team groups UV the cloth template.
- *`_e` glow:* unchanged; the `_e` file is a Blockbench texture with `render_mode: emissive` so the viewport shows it.
- *Relief `_r`:* unchanged; a Blockbench texture the build still composites and meshes (depth 3).
- *Outer `_o`:* unchanged.

### 2.3 Migration

1. `build_skins.py --to-bbmodel <troop>` writes a `.bbmodel` from today's JSON and PNGs (the reverse of the
   converter). It is for troops only; the six weapons are authored in Blockbench, not converted (Controller's call). Every Gilded
   troop gets one; the JSON geometry keys are deleted in the same commit. The output of the build is byte-identical
   before and after for troops (the `--verify` gate). Weapons get a new golden output, judged by eye on a side-by-side
   (Controller's call).
2. The loader is unchanged for troops (format 2 bones and relief) and gains format 3 for weapons. `FORMAT` becomes 3 in
   `index.json`; a troop model stays at its keys.
3. A House that has not been converted keeps its JSON geometry until its art half runs; the build accepts either source
   per troop (a `.bbmodel` beside the `.json` wins) during Phase 2 and refuses JSON geometry after Task 10.
4. `art/parts/*.json` stays as the part library. Parts as `.bbmodel` is parked, and parts stay JSON, checked against the troop's groups (Controller's call).

### 2.4 How the converter sits in `build_skins.py`

New module `tools/bbmodel.py` (pure, no Minecraft): `read(path) -> Model` (elements, groups with composed transforms,
textures with paths), `weapon_frames(model) -> {frame: [quad20]}`, `troop_bones(model, rig) -> bones as bake_bones
takes them`, `write(model, path)`. `build_skins.py` calls it from `weapon_outputs` and `bake`. It builds quads from a `.bbmodel` for weapons and troops, and
troops go the other way too; `--to-bbmodel` is troops only (Controller's call). Tests in
`test_build_skins.py` on fixture `.bbmodel` files: a rotated cube's corners, group composition, the y flip and
winding, a missing frame group, a cube at the root, a texture with the wrong UV size, a bone that is not in the rig.

## 4. Texture rules, in short

The full guide is `docs/ART-STYLE-GUIDE.md`. In one breath: five tones per material from one hue-shifted ramp (shadows
toward blue, lights toward warm yellow, 6 degrees a step, 22% value a step); one light from the top left; faces painted
by direction (up 4, north and west 3, south 2, east 1, down 0; polished metal's big faces one up); one pattern per
material (grain, sheen, gilt edge, weave or bands, leather pits, bone crack); the team tint rule as a ramp clamp; a
never-list (no sixth tone, no gradients, no stretched texel, no highlight on a whole face). 1 texel per model px, on
weapons as on skins. The spike's ramps for steel, gilt, wood, linen, leather and bowstring are in the guide.

## 6. LabPBR maps

**Naming.** Beside every shipped colour texture: `<name>_n.png` and `<name>_s.png`, the same size. That is
`textures/entity/troop/<troop>_s<stage>_n.png`, `_s<stage>_s.png`, `_veteran_n/_s`, `weapon/<id>_n.png`, `weapon/<id>_s.png`.
Not for `_e` (emission lives in `_s` alpha), `_o` (the outer twin is a cutout over the skin; its own maps would fight
the skin's), or the team cloth sheets (cloth is matte; a flat default reads right and saves 8 files).

**Layout (LabPBR 1.3, shaderlabs.org/wiki/LabPBR_Materials):**
- `_n`: R, G the normal's x and y, DirectX convention (Y-), 128/128 flat; B ambient occlusion (255 none); A height
  (255 top, 1 deepest; never 0).
- `_s`: R perceptual smoothness (roughness = (1 - R/255)^2); G F0 0..229 linear (dielectrics about 8 to 12), 230 iron,
  231 gold, 232 aluminium, 233 chrome, 234 copper, 235 lead, 236 platinum, 237 silver, 255 albedo-as-F0; B porosity
  0..64 or subsurface 65..255; A emission 0..254, with 255 meaning none.

**Which assets, and how they are made.** Painters never paint `_n`/`_s`. For a weapon, the painter script
writes a material map `<name>_m.png` in flat key colours. For a troop, nobody paints the `_m` (Controller's call): the maps
`<troop>_s<stage>_m.png` and `<troop>_s<stage>_r_m.png` sit beside their colour sheets and are generated by script from
them, by ramp lookup through `art/ramps.json`, which maps each ramp to a material. The builder may extend the table
below with materials such as flesh, hair, eyes, verdigris, rust and kelp, with smoothness and F0 values from the LabPBR
1.3 reference or a cited source. The Houses inherit this naming. From the `_m`, the build derives both maps:

| key colour | material | `_s` R smooth | G F0 | B |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `#FF0000` | steel, polished | 185 | 230 (iron) | 0 |
| `#AA0000` | iron, dull plate, mail | 110 | 230 | 0 |
| `#FFAA00` | gilt, brass | 200 | 231 (gold) | 0 |
| `#8B4513` | wood | 70 | 10 | 40 (porosity) |
| `#FFFFFF` | linen, cloth, bowstring | 30 | 8 | 60 |
| `#553322` | leather, hide | 60 | 10 | 30 |
| `#EEEEDD` | bone | 90 | 12 | 20 |
| `#00FF00` | glass, water, slime | 240 | 20 | 200 (subsurface) |
| transparent | no material: the texel is transparent in the colour sheet | 0 | 0 | 0 |

The `_s` alpha is 255 everywhere except texels lit in `_e`, which get 200 (so Complementary's labPBR mode lights them
as the glow pass does; both paths stay). The `_n` is flat (128, 128), AO 255, with the height channel from the style
guide's pattern heights (grain lines and weave 0.4 to 0.6 of a texel) and a one-texel normal tilt along every patch
edge of a relief or a blade bevel, which the build already knows from the mesh. Smoothness rises 12 per tone above the
face tone, so a highlight glints. The spike's `paint.py` makes exactly these for the two weapons (side-by-side.png, row 3).

**Build checks** (`build_skins.py`, exit 1 naming the file and texel):
- `_m` is the colour sheet's size and every opaque colour texel has a key colour, every transparent one none;
- a key colour not in the table, or on a troop a colour that matches no ramp in `art/ramps.json` (Controller's call);
- `_n` alpha of 0 anywhere (LabPBR's POM breaks on it);
- `_s` G in 238..254 (undefined metals);
- an emission texel outside the `_e` layer, or `_e` lit texels over the glow cap (today's check);
- `--verify` compares the generated maps like any other PNG.

**Iris finding (settled from source, Iris 1.20.1 branch, the modpack's 1.7.6 line).** Iris loads `_n` and `_s` for
*every* texture of class `SimpleTexture` (Yarn `ResourceTexture`), not only atlas sprites:
`IrisRenderingPipeline.onSetShaderTexture` -> `PBRTextureManager.getOrLoadHolder(id)` -> `PBRTextureLoaderRegistry`
(`SimpleTexture` -> `SimplePBRLoader`, `TextureAtlas` -> `AtlasPBRLoader`) -> `SimplePBRLoader.createPBRTexture`, which
builds `location.withPath(PBRType::appendSuffix)` (`_n`, `_s` before the extension) and loads it as a `SimpleTexture`,
returning null when the file is missing (then the flat defaults `0x7F7FFFFF` / `0x00000000` are bound). Our renderer
binds `textures/entity/troop/...png` through `RenderLayer.getEntityCutout(Identifier)`, which goes through
`TextureManager.bindTexture` and creates a `ResourceTexture` for a plain identifier, so our troop and weapon textures
get their maps with no code change. The load happens on first bind under a shader pipeline, so a resource reload is
enough to pick up new maps.

**Complementary caveat.** Complementary Reimagined r5.5.1 reads the maps on entities only in its labPBR mode:
`shaders/lib/common.glsl` has `#define RP_MODE 1 //[1 0 3 2]` (1 = Integrated PBR+, the default; 3 = labPBR), and
`gbuffers_entities.glsl` calls `GetCustomMaterials` (which samples `normals` and `specular`) only under `CUSTOM_PBR`,
which `RP_MODE >= 2` defines. In the default mode the maps are ignored and the shader makes up materials from colour.
So the maps show for players who set "RP Support: labPBR" in the shader's options, and for the harness run when
`shaderpacks/ComplementaryReimagined_r5.5.1.zip.txt` holds `RP_MODE=3`. Nothing else is needed; no outside mod.

**Kort's ruling (2026-10-21, questions round 25), K3: Complementary's mode.** Complementary only reads our `_n` / `_s` maps in labPBR mode (`RP_MODE=3`), and
that mode turns off its built-in PBR (Integrated PBR+) for every vanilla block. Kort ruled: ship the maps and leave the
modpack's Complementary on its default mode, with the maps used only by players who switch. The other two options,
setting the modpack's Complementary to labPBR and skipping the maps, were not taken.

## 7. Workflow

**An agent, through the MCP plugin** (`.superpowers/sdd/2026-10-17-phase-2-houses/bbmcp.py`, Blockbench open):
1. Paint the sheets with the painter script under `tools/art/` (the spike's `paint.py` carried in: it allocates one
   patch per face and paints by the style guide from a cube list with materials) or by hand with Pillow, by the guide.
   The painter scripts are first-pass helpers: once a `.bbmodel` exists, it is the source, and Blockbench edits win
   (Controller's call).
2. `create_project {name: <id>, format: free}`; `create_texture {name, width, height, uv_width, uv_height, data: <data URL>}`.
3. `add_group` per frame; `place_cube` per cube with `faces: [{face, uv}]` in texel units, `group`, `texture`.
4. `create_offscreen_view` (1024, no antialias, `copy_view: none`), `set_camera_angle {view, position, target, projection:
   perspective, fov: 70}` at 96 px for in-game size and 24 px enlarged, `capture_screenshot {view}`; judge against the
   guide.
5. Read the live file: `resources/read blockbench://project/<uuid>.bbmodel`, write it to `art/weapons/<id>.bbmodel`
   (`export_model` with a `path` prompts Kort for file access; the resource read does not).
6. `python tools/build_skins.py`, then the suite.

The spike's `bb.py` does steps 2 to 5 in 100 lines; the quirks it met are in the report (view ids are `view` not `id`
on delete; `delete_offscreen_view` disposes the view then errors "onContextMenu is not defined"; `pbr_channel` on
`create_texture` needs a `group`; Blockbench's PBR material is Bedrock MER, not LabPBR, and the offscreen view does not
render it; `export_model` to a path and `create_texture` from a file path need Kort's permission, data URLs do not).

**Kort, by hand:** open `art/weapons/<id>.bbmodel` or `art/troops/<troop>.bbmodel` in Blockbench 5.2.2. Model in the
groups; paint in Paint mode on the linked PNG; save (Ctrl+S writes the `.bbmodel`; the texture's own save writes the
PNG, and the build's `--verify` catches a forgotten one). Run `python tools/build_skins.py` and the suite, or hand it
to an agent. A troop file's bone groups are not locked: their cubes and pivots are authoritative, and the build checks only names,
parents and rest rotation against the rig (section 2.2).

## 8. Budgets

- **Weapon quads:** cap 128 a frame (depth 2, unchanged). Guide: 8 to 16 cubes a frame; at most 21. The Gilded
  spike: sword 66, bow 72 to 78. The 5d sprites draw 16 (sword) and 22 to 46 (bow), so a cube weapon costs about 40
  quads more a troop. At 152 troops that is about 7,000 quads, 0.1 to 0.3 ms by depth 5's vertex cost, on top of the relief's
  5,000 to 9,600.
- **Depth budget (Controller's call):** the depth budget stays, +1.0 ms at the ground camera and +0.3 ms at the commander camera,
  and now covers relief and weapons together.
- **Fourth cut, in scope (Controller's call):** a weapon on a troop outside the near set (beyond `NEAR_BLOCKS` or past `NEAR_CAP`)
  draws a far version of at most 32 quads, built automatically from the `rest` group (section 3). Near troops draw the
  full cube model.
- **Cost check and fallback (Controller's call):** `p2-depth-cost` gates on - flat at both cameras with the far versions on. If a
  camera is still over budget, a new first cut cuts the weapon near distance from 24 to 16; the relief cuts (depth
  section 5) follow after it.
- **Relief:** unchanged (256 a stage, 20% a face).
- **Textures:** section 5. Jar growth: 6 weapons x 3 maps x 2 KB plus 30 troops x 3 stages x 2 maps x about 6 KB, under
  1.5 MB.
- **Loading:** the 20-number parse is the 13-number parse plus a cross product; a few ms a reload as today.
