# Lore reboot: the giants' land

Status: approved by Kort, 2026-11-09. Lore bible and chapter 1 outline drafted in `docs/drafts/` (`lore-bible-2026-11-09.md`, `chapter-1-outline-2026-11-09.md`). Written from a brainstorm with Kort the same day. It replaces the lore of `2026-10-18-campaign-story-design.md` (the Long March, the Barrowlands, the Horn, Edric Vane) and the House flavour text in `2026-10-17-houses-design.md` section 1.2. Nothing is built. Calls I made alone are marked **[call]**; everything else is Kort's decision.

## 1. Intent and why

Kort wants the world, the five Houses and the campaign to share one reason for fighting, with an aesthetic that matches. The old lore is retired in full, including the Graveborn virus origin Kort first proposed and then dropped. The new spine:

- The land is the bodies of earthen giants.
- Their drained mana, glimpur, gave every other living thing its magic.
- The giants are waking and want all glimpur destroyed.
- The saga runs as a three-act arc (section 9). 1.0.0 is season 1: the Houses fight one another over old wrongs, the giants are only hinted at, and almost nothing is explained. In season 2 the first elder wakes and the giants arrive as the enemy. In season 3 the rest wake, the saga resolves, and the giants become a playable House.
- The saga's question is why anything should go on living, harm and all. The giants chose to stop out of boredom. The Houses, petty, furious and alive, are the answer.

## 2. Goals, non-goals, anti-goals

Goals:
- One cosmology that explains why each House exists, why they fight, and where magic comes from.
- Every House is both wronged and wrongdoer, so no chapter is a clean side.
- A player who finishes the launch campaign suspects the land is alive before anyone says so.
- Giants who are credible as season 2's enemy and sympathetic enough to be a playable House later.
- A long-term spine that later seasons build on without contradicting season 1: every season 1 hint pays off later (section 9), and season 1 explains almost nothing.

Non-goals:
- Giant units or bosses in 1.0.0.
- Any plague, virus or hivemind.
- Changes to House mechanics, rosters, counters, traits or Legendary stats.
- A branching story.

Anti-goals (a failure even if it works):
- Giants as cartoon villains. A player agreeing with them about the Gilded is the design working. A player agreeing with them about everyone else means the writing failed.
- A reveal that makes earlier chapters a lie.
- Lore that exists only in a document, not in the game.

## 3. Constraints

- Binding from the shipped game: the five Houses, their sheets (troops, traits, counters), the five Legendary names (Lord Marshal Aldric, the Hollow King, Old Horn, Brassjaw, Admiral Vael), and the shipped map names (Rivergate, Ironhold, Frostmarch, Hollowwood, Kingsfield). Mechanics do not change.
- The player picks any House per match in skirmish and server play, with no story reason needed: choosing a House means being one of that species. Campaign chapters fix the House for that chapter's story battles.
- Giants appear in 1.0.0 only as hints: tremors, ebbing glimpur, one uncovered eye. No giant is a unit.

## 4. The world

**The land.** I call it **Eldmark**, a human name for a land far older than they know **[call: placeholder, Kort decides]**. Its mountains, plains and sea floor are the bodies of the earthen giants. Every human act on it happened on a corpse.

**The giants and the spire.** The giants grew so advanced that they became bored of being alive. They built a spire that drained their mana, and their bodies crumbled into the land. The mana leaked out as **glimpur**, which now gives magic to every living thing. Aeons later the spire reactivated and the giants are waking, calmly, and want to redeem their lost land.

**What the giants want and why they frighten.** They want all glimpur destroyed so it can never be used for harm. They are right that it has been used for harm, above all by the Gilded realms. They are monstrous because:
- Graveborn bones, Tidecaller minds, Forge machines and Wildmoot cunning all run on glimpur. The cure is extinction, and the giants do not count the Houses as people, only as residue.
- They threw the mana away themselves, out of boredom, and now claim to guard it. The Houses' rebuttal: you gave up the right when you left it lying.

**The Skeleton King as their example.** What angers them most is his binding: the dead are meant to be laid to rest, and he used glimpur to keep them walking. They see him as proof of what the Houses do with magic. They do not kill him at once. Once all the elders are awake they use him as the example to the Houses (section 9).

Their voice is calm, courteous and inevitable, with a violet-white glow in the mana they pull. They offer terms to the Gilded alone (see "Who the cure kills"). They never raise their voice.

**Glimpur.** The giants' drained mana, now in everything. Its sign in the world is a violet-white shimmer. When the spire pulls it home, magic ebbs.

**The giants' civilization.** Machine and crystal: vast clockwork and glimpur-crystal engines. The forge people's redstone is a fragment of giant technology, which makes the Ironclad the giants' nearest kin.

**The elders.** The giants are regional elders, each body a different part of the land. **[call]** Five lie on Eldmark: one under the Gilded capital (the leader, and the first to wake), and one each under Thruggsen, Tengervald, the plains and the sea floor. More lie beyond the sea, in Demacrus and other continents, which later seasons can use. They share one will, led by the first to wake, until the Houses break it (section 9).

**The leader elder** (Kort: the weary one). The most tired of the giants. It wakes because the spire pulled it back, not because it wants to be alive, and the cure is its grief rather than its crusade. Persuading it that life is worth living is the saga's emotional ending (section 9).

**Elder names** (Kort chose giant-tongue names with translations). **[call: the names and translations below are my placeholders, Kort vetoes]** They are slow, deep names with long vowels. The Houses' folk myths (section 8) are distorted memories of these same elders.

| Elder | Body | Name | Translation | House myth |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Leader | under the Gilded capital | Orruvan | Counted Enough | Gilded scholars dismiss it as old stone |
| Mountains | under Thruggsen | Kharuun | Furnace Heart | Forge: the Makers |
| Mountains | under Tengervald | Ysmorren | Quiet Under Snow | Graveborn: the Rest |
| Plains | the grasslands | Ommeru | Wide Breath | Wildmoot: the Great Herd Beneath |
| Sea | the sea floor | Thalluun | Deep Mother | Tidecallers: the Deep Mother (their myth keeps the translation) |

Nobody speaks these names until season 2 at the earliest, and the player first hears Orruvan's.

**Why the spire woke.** The Skeleton King's binding was the first great misuse of glimpur and crossed a threshold that tripped the spire. The humans caused their own reckoning.

**Where the spire is.** Under the Gilded capital. The human realms built their greatest city on the leader elder's chest.

**Who the cure kills.** Graveborn, Tidecallers, Forge and Wildmoot depend on glimpur and would die or revert. Humans depend on it only for magic, so the giants can offer them terms: give up magic and stay. That temptation is the human thread of season 2.

**Terms are for the Gilded only** (Kort). Every other House is, in the giants' eyes, an outcome of glimpur used for evil, and the giants will not leave anything like that alive. No tailored temptation is offered to Wildmoot, Tidecallers, Ironclad or Graveborn; they face the cure with no terms. **[call]** One consequence: the Ironclad's redstone is giant technology, so some Forge hope the giants will treat them as kin, and are refused. That refusal is Forge's season 2 split.

## 5. The five Houses

Each House gets a species, a rewritten origin, one wrong done to it and one wrong it did, its Legendary as chapter lead, and the hint of the giants its chapter shows.

| House | Species | Legendary |
|---|---|---|
| Gilded Order | human (the realms' army) | Lord Marshal Aldric |
| Wildmoot | animals | Old Horn, the Antlered Alpha |
| Ironclad | forge people: redstone and machine | Brassjaw, the Forge Colossus |
| Tidecallers | sea and water people | Admiral Vael, the Drowned Tide |
| Graveborn | rebels from the Skeleton King's army | the Hollow King |

**Gilded Order.** Humans who came from Demacrus and founded the realms, a confederacy that shares one capital and uses the Gilded Order as its army. The capital stands on the leader elder's chest, above the spire. The Order is sworn to the Charter, the confederacy's founding document, which promises to protect all its people. In practice it excluded animals and the sea, so the oath was hollow before the Skeleton King broke it from inside. They hunted the Wildmoot for centuries, and their mages bombarded the sea. The Skeleton King was one of their kings, so the realms are complicit in the first great misuse of glimpur. Wronged by: Tidecallers, who drowned their coast towns. Did wrong to: Wildmoot. Hint: spells sputter, and a quarry uncovers a giant's eye.

**Wildmoot.** Grazing animals, not built for fighting, who hid when humans began hunting them and adapted over centuries into something fierce and bloodthirsty for humans. **[call]** They grazed grass rooted in giant flesh and rich in glimpur, which is why centuries of hunting made them cunning rather than extinct. Their tone is loud, feral grief and vengeance. They do not get the calm dread, which belongs to the giants. Wronged by: Gilded. Did wrong to: Forge, whose mining caravans they raided. Hint: herds flee uphill, and the grass withers.

**Ironclad (the forge people).** A clan of expert geologists from Demacrus, among the first humans to land. They settled the mountains of Thruggsen and over centuries used all of it. They tapped redstone veins, which are glimpur crystallised in giant bone, and grafted it into themselves and their mechs until their descendants became half machine. Wronged by: Wildmoot. Did wrong to: Tidecallers, whose rivers their smelters poisoned. Hint: redstone veins pulse toward the mountains, and the forges lose heat.

**Tidecallers.** Marine life that was once only fish, sharks and shrimp. Mages with no safe place to experiment bombarded the ocean with fire, lightning and poison. The sea's glimpur remembered the human casters and remade the marine life in their image. **[call: Kort's original pitch had the glimpur appear as a trace of the spells; with glimpur now the giants' mana, I have the spells churning the sea's own glimpur, which already lay in the sea floor.]** Painful, but it made them furious and ready to avenge their water. Wronged by: Ironclad. Did wrong to: Gilded, whose coast towns they drowned. Hint: the tide pulls inland, and their glow fades.

**Graveborn.** The bound dead of the Skeleton King's army who broke the binding this year. The other Houses distrust them because they were once part of it, and because what the rebels did while bound is remembered. They are the first sign of the giants: the spire's first pulse began pulling glimpur home, which frayed the king's binding, and the dead woke up. The rebels do not know why they can think again. Their leader is the Skeleton King's defected general, the first bound and the first free, whom the rebels mockingly call the Hollow King. Wronged by: the Skeleton King, and the suspicion of everyone. Did wrong to: Wildmoot and Tidecallers, while bound: the rebels remember razing Wildmoot dens and sinking Tidecaller ships **[call: Kort chose the idea of wrongs done under the binding; the specific victims are mine]**. The others' distrust is therefore partly earned, and the chapter is about owning it, which is why the Graveborn close the campaign. Hint: the binding frays, and the rebellion itself is the hint.

## 6. The Skeleton King

A human king of one of the confederacy's realms (Tengervald, which the others looked away from) who learned to bind the dead with glimpur: the first great misuse, and the one that gives the giants a legitimate grievance. He leads a hostile NPC army, not playable, which still hunts the rebels. In 1.0.0 he is the enemy with a leader and the launch finale's one fight every House can join.

- **Motive: to protect his people** (Kort). Tengervald was dying and the confederacy looked away, so he bound the dead to defend his realm and kept going long past the point of decency. The Charter's failure made him, and he is tragic as well as guilty. That is why the giants' execution of him in season 3 should land as both deserved and sad.
- **[call]** How he learned binding is player-facing in chapter 5, told by the Hollow King, who served him. Earlier chapters carry only archive items.
- **In season 2 he keeps hunting the Houses** (Kort), so the Houses fight two enemies at once: the giants and the bound army. The giants judge him only in season 3.
- He survives season 1 and is the saga's recurring enemy. The giants kill him once all the elders are awake, before the Houses, as an example of what the Houses' harm earns. **[call]** I read Kort's "living throughout the chapters" as seasons 1 and 2, with his death in season 3.
- **[call]** He is unnamed in this document. Kort or the chapter writer names him.
- **[call]** His realm is **Tengervald**, one of Kort's two mountain names, now put to use as the harsh mountain kingdom where he first bound the dead. Thruggsen stays the Ironclad's mountains.

## 7. Timeline

**[call]** All dates are my guess and are for consistency, not for display.
- Ages ago: the giants build the spire and crumble.
- About 900 years ago: Ironclad's ancestors land from Demacrus, then the realms follow.
- Following centuries: the Gilded hunt the Wildmoot, and the mages bombard the sea.
- About 150 years ago: the Skeleton King rises in Tengervald and binds the dead.
- This year: the Graveborn rebel, because the spire has woken and frayed the binding. The 1.0.0 campaign begins.

## 8. The campaign shape (1.0.0)

Five chapters, each played from that House's side, each led by its Legendary. The battles are the House's grievances. They run in ring order, ending with the Graveborn:

1. Gilded (Aldric)
2. Wildmoot (Old Horn)
3. Ironclad (Brassjaw)
4. Tidecallers (Vael)
5. Graveborn (the Hollow King)

**[call]** Each chapter opens on the wrong the previous House did and ends on the wrong the chapter's House commits. Chapter 1 opens on the drowned coast, and chapter 4 shows the drowning from the Tidecallers' side, so the ring closes.

Chapter 5 is the finale: the Skeleton King's army is the one fight the Houses must join, grudgingly, beside the distrusted Graveborn. The army breaks, but the Skeleton King survives and withdraws **[call]**. The last scene hints at the spire's light and sets up season 2.

Hints build chapter by chapter in the order in section 5, so by the finale the pattern is plain: glimpur is leaving.

**The ring stays open** (Kort). Chapter 5 ends with the Houses still wronged and still wary, joined only against the Skeleton King's army. Season 2 is what breaks that pressure.

**The commander is a silent blank** (Kort). The player has no name, face or lines. Picking a House means being that species, so the player speaks through the House and its Legendary.

**[call] Whose army the player fights.** In each chapter the early battles are against the House that wronged the chapter's House. The later battles are the chapter's House committing its own wrong against the next House in the ring. The player therefore sees both sides of every grievance. Chapter 5's enemy is the Skeleton King's army. That he was a Gilded king is a fact of Gilded guilt, shown in chapter 1's archive items and again in chapter 5, not a separate Gilded battle.

**[call] Order.** Chapters unlock in ring order and later chapters may assume earlier ones. Kort chose a fixed order over player-picked order.

**[call] Speech.** All five Houses share a common tongue and count as people. Among the Wildmoot, the speakers are the boar-kin hunters and Old Horn; hounds, spiders and bears do not speak. The giants do not count the Houses as people.

**[call] How overt the hints are.** In chapters 1 to 4 nobody names the giants. Characters notice the symptom and blame something of their own: the Gilded blame sabotage, Forge blames its mines, the Tidecallers blame the moon. The finale's spire light is the first moment the game itself says the land is alive.

**Seeds planted in 1.0.0** (Kort chose these two): giant relics on maps (statues, a stone hand, a carved eye) so the land looks like it is looking back, and glimpur ebbing in play. **[call]** The ebb is a visual and audio tell on magic troops in later chapters (flicker, dimmer glow) with no mechanical effect, because House mechanics are not changing and gameplay changes need Kort's approval. An envoy's terms and giant-age inscriptions were offered and not chosen.

**Folk memory (season 1).** Each House holds a distorted, half-true myth: Wildmoot tell of the Great Herd Beneath, Forge of the Makers, Tidecallers of the Deep Mother, and Gilded scholars dismiss it all as old stone. **[call]** The Graveborn's myth is the Rest: the dead are owed a rest they were denied.

## 9. The long-term arc

The saga ends when the giants choose to live and share glimpur (the cycle breaks), not when anyone wins a war.

| Season | Job | What the player sees |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (1.0.0) | The Houses fight over old wrongs. The giants are hinted and almost nothing is explained. | Five House chapters, relics on maps, ebbing glimpur, the spire's light at the end. |
| 2 | The leader elder wakes under the capital and the giants arrive as the enemy. | The capital besieged. The giants as a non-playable enemy. The Houses split: Gilded are offered the only terms, because humans lose only magic (Lord Marshal Aldric is the likeliest to accept, and his oath to the Charter is both his reason to refuse and his reason to accept), Forge is divided and refused when it hopes to be treated as kin, and Wildmoot, Tidecallers and Graveborn fight with no terms offered. |
| 3 | The other elders wake with one will. They execute the Skeleton King before the Houses as an example of what harm earns. The will fractures as the Houses prove life has worth, and the saga resolves with the weary leader, Orruvan, choosing to live. | The remaining Eldmark elders, then elders beyond the sea. The giants become a playable House. |
| After | New arcs on a settled world. | Demacrus and other giant bodies, new peoples. |

What each season 1 hint pays off:
- Relics on maps: the elders' bodies.
- Ebbing glimpur: the spire pulling it home, which the giants start in season 2.
- The Graveborn rebellion: the spire's first pulse, which explains why the dead can think.
- The Gilded quarry's eye: the leader elder under the capital.
- Forge's pulsing veins: the giants' machine heritage.
- Tidecaller tides and Wildmoot herds: the land waking.

Rules for later seasons: nothing may contradict a season 1 hint, and season 1 never names the giants before its finale. **[call]** How the Houses prove life has worth is left to season 3's writer.

## 10. Aesthetic

- The shipped House silhouettes and troop rosters stay. Only palettes, markings and flavour move.
- **Wildmoot:** keep the boar-kin, wolf, spider and antler look. Loud, feral, no violet.
- **Ironclad:** soot iron and brass shift toward redstone-red glow and visible machine. Their existing mobs (vindicator, pillager, iron golem) stay.
- **Tidecallers:** unchanged beyond the origin.
- **Graveborn:** bone and rusted iron stay, with the broken binding shown as cut cords or cracked sigils.
- **Giants:** stone and earth, calm, with a violet-white glimpur glow. Not in battle in 1.0.0.
- The five House gear sets in `docs/drafts/house-gear-sets-2026-11-04.md` keep their names; Forgewall's palette will need a redstone pass **[call]**.
