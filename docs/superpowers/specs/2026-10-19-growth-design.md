# The growth design: from a modpack to a game you cannot put down

Status: design approved by Kort 2026-10-19 (the growth plan was approved as written;
the six calls in section 19 are mine to keep or overturn). This spec extends
`docs/VISION-AND-ROADMAP.md` from 0.5.0 to 1.0.0. It does not replace it: the
pillars, the workflow and every standing rule stay. Where this document and an
older plan disagree about the future, this document wins; where it and a spec
for work already in flight disagree, the older spec wins until its phase ships.

Sources read for this design: `PROJECT-STATE.md`, `VISION-AND-ROADMAP.md`,
`PARKED.md`, the Houses spec, the built-world spec, the UI spec, the unit-AI
spec, the campaign story spec, the Phase 4 modes design, the Phase 2, 3 and 4
plans, `sheets/design.json` on `phase-1-solo-core` and `phase-2-houses`, and the
code it names.

---

## 1. Intent and why

The game has more content than most modpacks ever get: 30 troops across five
Houses, 20 counter rules, 27 abilities, 11 orders and 6 stances planned, four
modes, five hand-built maps, five chapters of story and a 30-tier chapter. What
it does not have is **memory**. Nothing remembers what the player did, nothing
the player owns is at risk, and every battlefield plays in the same weather.

This design adds three spines, in this order:

1. **Attachment.** Three troops in every banner get a name, and a name can be
   lost. The dead a player leaves on a map stand up on that map.
2. **Ritual.** A reason to open the game today that is not "play another match":
   Oaths, a front that moves while you are away, a rival who taunts you.
3. **Consequence.** The field changes the fight; the standard can be taken; the
   story's choices change the banner, not only the book.

Every system below reuses machinery that exists or is in flight. That is the
test it passed: no new engine, no new dependency, no new map before 1.0.

## 2. Goals and non-goals

Goals:
1. A player finishing chapter 1 can name their three sergeants and the battle
   they lost one in.
2. Every match type has a visible condition and a standard, so no two sittings
   feel identical.
3. A solo player moves a shared front, and a returning player is told what
   changed while they were gone.
4. The pass measures breadth of play, not time served; no tier reads "30 gold".
5. Every release ships with a screenshot-verified build and a Kort playtest.

Non-goals:
- New maps, new dimensions, mounts, naval combat, base building, an open world.
- Ranked matchmaking, paid tiers, paid currency, a second HUD.
- More than 30 troops, more than five Houses, more than six commander classes.
- A new dependency or a loader change. Minecraft 1.20.1 and Fabric only.
- Rewriting chapters 1 to 5. The story is written; this design changes its
  delivery and its consequences, not its plot.

Anti-goals:
- Depth by numbers. The counter web is not extended to make conditions "work";
  conditions multiply on top of it.
- Punishment as retention. Every loss system has a cheap undo and an opt-out.
- Fake scale. The front shows real counts from real matches, never invented
  numbers.

## 3. Constraints

- Minecraft 1.20.1, Fabric, Java 17. No new dependency.
- Numbers live in `sheets/design.json`. `Spec.java` is generated by
  `tools/preflight.py` and never hand-edited. Every row has every column; a
  double needs a decimal point in the first row; a sheet with no rows is an
  error, so a sheet lands in the task that gives it its first rows.
- One `Battle` per server. Meta systems (front, barrow, oaths) are profile or
  server-JSON state, never a second world.
- Harness code lives only in `test-tools/` and joins the `forbidden` tuple in
  `tools/release_config.py`.
- Packet layouts: a changed layout bumps its channel id; host and client run the
  same build.
- Pillar 5 binds: nothing is done until a running game shows it by log line and
  screenshot, and the tester states in one sentence what the screenshot shows.
- One working rule from the story spec applies to every player-facing string
  below: ASCII, no dashes as punctuation, no exclamation marks, feed lines fit
  140 px, cards wrap to three lines.
- Never publish without Kort's go. Park cut ideas, never delete them.

## 5. S1: Conditions

### 5.1 What it is

Every match has one condition, shown before deployment and during the battle,
that changes shots, movement and light. Six conditions: calm, rain, fog, night,
blizzard, gale. It is the game's weather, which today cannot happen:
`GravesAndGlory.java:243` pins `setWeather(0,0,false,false)` and the Houses
spec's Tidecallers trait reads "while it rains on the map".

### 5.2 Rules

- The `conditions` sheet (section 15.1) holds the numbers. `calm` is the default
  and changes nothing.
- One multiplier stage: `Combat.resolve` multiplies the condition factor after
  the counters, traits, auras and commander factors and before the clamp, so
  C1 to C18c keep their exact meaning. `HitLog` gains a `conditions` field so a
  hit can be explained.
- Movement: `Tactics.walk` multiplies its speed by `Conditions.move(id)`.
- Morale: `Morale.tick` adds `Conditions.routDelta(id)` to the share it compares.
- Rise Again: `Abilities`' Rise Again roll adds `Conditions.riseBonus(id)` points
  to its chance (night only), capped at the House trait's tier III value.
- Setup: at `startRound` the server sets the world time (`night`), the weather
  (`rain`), and the client fog density. Teardown restores the world clock and
  weather to the values the round found, exactly as Phase 4's mode teardown
  restores its pieces.
- Where it comes from: Skirmish has a `Field` row in the picker (`Any`, `Calm`,
  `Rain`, `Fog`, `Night`, `Blizzard`, `Gale`); `Any` rolls by the sheet weights;
  Practice is always `calm`; a campaign battle takes its own row's condition,
  fixed; Survival is `night`; the lobby's pads show it.
- Tidecallers: their rain clause reads `world.isRaining()`, which the condition
  sets, so no new trait code is needed.
- Bots read the same maths through `Combat`, so no bot logic changes. `Hard` may
  prefer its House's favourite condition when it picks the field for a bot-hosted
  match (a `botCommand` column, draft).

### 5.3 Why it serves the pillars

Readable drama: a condition is one sentence a player can hold in their head, and
it shows in the sky. Your banner is yours: the same banner in rain and in fog
plays differently. Built, not generated: no map is generated to get the variety.
Solo is a real game: the campaign's authored weather becomes real. Honest
numbers: every number is a sheet cell.

### 5.4 Acceptance

- A rain match measurably shortens bow kills and speeds Tidecallers.
- The field card names the condition before deployment; the ledger shows its
  seal; the report records it.
- Each chapter battle's condition equals its scene's weather (fog at the river,
  night at the shrine, blizzard at the pass, night at Kingsfield).
- Twenty sims per condition keep equal-banner win rates inside 40 to 60 per cent.

## 6. S2: The Standard

### 6.1 What it is

Each side's standard stands on the field at its own base. While it stands it
steadies the line; when it falls it can be carried home for a swing of score and
morale. It is not a second win condition.

### 6.2 Rules (Domination in 0.6; the sheet is per mode)

- One standard per side at `Battle.fieldBase(team)`, an armour stand implementing
  `ArmyMob` and carrying the class pattern of `ArmySupplyBanner`, tagged
  `standard`. The client draws the team's cloth and pennant from `Spec.FACTIONS`.
- `standards.hp` 80 (draft). Damage: melee 1.0, arrow and shot 0.25, siege 4.0,
  magic 1.0, all from the row's multipliers, applied in `Combat.resolve` when the
  target is a standard. No counter row is added.
- Aura: own groups whose centre is within `auraRadius` 20 (draft) get
  `routDelta -0.05` and `regroupBonusSeconds` 5 (draft) added to their regroup
  immunity. `Morale.tick` adds it per group through one call.
- At 0 HP the standard becomes `down` and drops a carrier object at its feet,
  reusing Phase 4's `BannerMode` pickup, carry, drop and return code (the same
  carrier helmet, glow, speed and no-attack rules).
- An enemy carrier takes it to its own standard's base and scores
  `scoreValue` 40 (draft) and applies `routDelta +0.10` to the owner for 60 s.
  The owner's touch returns it at once; untouched for `dropReturnSeconds` 120
  (draft) it returns home.
- Teardown restores: the standard is discarded, the carrier helmet, glow and
  speed are cleared, and a round that ends while it is carried leaves nothing
  behind (the Phase 4 teardown rules, extended).
- Siege, Survival and Banner take rows later; their own objects (the gate, the
  shrine, the banner) are already the standard for that mode.
- Bots: one infantry group guards its standard when an enemy group is within 40
  and no point is contested; on Hard a cavalry group raids the enemy standard
  when the enemy is fully committed. A new `botCommand` column
  `standardGuard` (easy false, normal true, hard true) and a `doctrines` column
  `raidStandard` (false for Gilded and Ironclad, true for Graveborn, Wildmoot and
  Tidecallers) carry the judgement, not a new AI.

### 6.3 Why it serves the pillars

The game is called Graves and Glory and is about banners; the standard makes the
title a rule. It gives every match a thing to protect and a comeback that is
visible from across the field. It reuses the carrier and structure code Phase 4
already pays for.

### 6.4 Acceptance

- Stealing a standard visibly swings a match the player was losing.
- The feed names every state: down, carried, home, broken.
- A player who never opens a tooltip can say what happened.
- A round that ends mid-carry leaves no helmet, glow, or speed behind.

## 7. S3: Sergeants and the Roll of the Dead

### 7.1 What it is

Three troops in every banner get names and a small command presence. When one
falls, the name goes in a book and the slot gets a new, nameless soldier.

### 7.2 Rules

- At deployment each group with three or more troops promotes a sergeant: highest
  stars, then highest evolve stage, then first in the lineup. He keeps his troop
  type and gets `+10%` health (draft), a brass chevron above his head (client
  render only), a name from the `sergeants` sheet, and a `+5%` damage aura to his
  group within 8 blocks (draft).
- On death: his group's `steadyUntil` resets (a shock, never a rout), one feed
  line names him, and a `roll` entry is written: name, troop, map, mode, match
  id, day.
- After the battle a successor is promoted from the highest-star survivor. He
  takes the slot, never the name. The name is the thing that was lost.
- Campaign: the death is permanent. Skirmish: the sergeant is carried from the
  field and returns next battle (names do not die in practice). Practice: no
  sergeants. The mode's rule is a `sergeants` sheet column (`deathRule`
  `permanent` or `carried`), not code.
- Milestones: `sergeants` sheet rows by battles fought (10, 25, 50): title
  Veteran, Ensign, Captain; a sash cosmetic at 25; at 50 a byname and a line in
  the roll while alive. The roll of the dead is read with Pell in the archive, and
  the graveyard shrine gains one stone per entry.
- Names come from `sergeants` (24 rows, names and bynames in the copy's
  register). The copy writer owns the list; the task never invents a name.

### 7.3 Data

Profile schema 4 (section 15.3): `sergeants` (three records), `roll` (list). Pure
`Sergeant.java` (promotion, records, milestones) and `Roll.java` (render data).
The match ledger already counts kills and survivals per troop, so no new counter
is needed; the sergeant record is a small addition to the finish path.

### 7.4 Acceptance

- After five battles the player can name the three sergeants.
- A sergeant's death is felt: the feed, the roll, the shrine stone, and a
  successor with no name.
- In Skirmish nobody's name is ever lost.

## 8. S4: The Barrow Debt

### 8.1 What it is

The troops a player loses on a map rise there in the next match on that map.
Burying them costs gold and closes the loop. It is the game's title as a rule,
its gold sink, and the mechanic that makes preserving troops matter.

### 8.2 Rules

- The profile keeps `barrow`: map id to a list of troop ids and counts, capped at
  12 per map. Every loss in a finished round is added; a win and a loss both add.
- The next match on that map in Skirmish or campaign chapter 2 or later: at 90 s
  (draft) a risen group stands up at the map's `barrowSites` row, sized
  `ceil(lost/4)` capped at 6, built from the lost troop types as Graveborn
  (`fearless`, Rise Again tier I), hostile to the player's team only. They do not
  contest points and never count for either side's score. A kill pays 1 gold and
  no XP.
- The Barrow Rite at the graveyard shrine costs 2 gold per dead (draft), clears
  that map's ledger, and pays 1 shard per 5 buried. It is the gold sink and the
  ritual.
- Off in Practice and in campaign chapter 1 (authored difficulty). On from
  chapter 2, with one line from Wren the first time it happens.
- A lost sergeant may rise in Skirmish only, after he is in the roll, with his
  name in the feed. Killing him again is the moment the mechanic exists for.
- Multiplayer: one shared ledger per map on the server, capped at 8, so the
  risen are the community's dead. No database: a server JSON beside the camp
  marker.
- Never raises companions. Never raises anything the script owns.

### 8.3 Why it serves the pillars

The dead not staying buried is the fiction's first sentence. It gives the economy
a sink that is not a price rise, makes losses mean something without permadeath
of the roster, and produces stories players repeat.

### 8.4 Acceptance

- After a bad loss on Hollowwood, the next match there has the player's dead
  standing at the ford, and the shrine offers to bury them for gold the player
  would rather spend on a recruit.
- Chapter 1 plays exactly as authored.
- Caps hold: six risen, no score, no XP, no companions.

## 9. S5: Oaths, House Chapters, Feats

### 9.1 What it is

The pass becomes a record of breadth. Daily and weekly contracts become Oaths
with mechanical conditions. Founders stays evergreen. Five House chapters carry
the long chase. Feats carry the bragging rights.

### 9.2 Rules

- `oaths` sheet (section 15.5): `id, kind, target, value, house, gold, shards,
  xp, text`. Kinds are a closed list: `win_mode, hold_points, no_losses,
  carry_standard, evolve, recruit_house, kill_legendary, survive_wave,
  win_condition`. Three drawn per day from the profile's day seed (deterministic,
  no server), three per week plus one Chapter Oath.
- XP for an Oath or a Feat is paid once. Repeats pay match XP only. The track
  measures breadth, not time served.
- `houseChapters`: 5 Houses by 12 tiers at 300 XP (draft), unlocked at Renown
  I, II, III. Rewards: shards six times, cosmetics three times (sash, frame,
  pattern), a guaranteed Rare, a guaranteed Epic at tier 12, one title. No gold
  tiers.
- `feats`: about 40 rows, cosmetic-only, including hard ones (a pure-House win,
  a Siege win with no siege troops, a match with the standard never lost, seven
  waves untouched). This absorbs the parked medal challenges.
- Founders keeps its 30 tiers and its never-expires rule; its gold and shard
  filler tiers move to cosmetics in the same rewrite.

### 9.3 Acceptance

- A week of play completes about two Oaths a day without grinding.
- No tier in any track reads "30 gold".
- The Chapter plaque becomes the Chase with tabs Founders, Houses, Feats.

## 10. S6: The Warfront

### 10.1 What it is

A seven-territory front in the Barrowlands that moves with finished matches, so
solo play changes a shared world.

### 10.2 Rules

- `warfront` sheet: territory rows with neighbours, a threshold and a reward.
- Each finished match adds front points to the Hollow's side; a weekly tick moves
  the front. The camp and the war table show the front, the player's own
  contribution, and the weekly aggregate.
- Solo reads its own contribution; with friends the server aggregates every
  player. No database before Phase 6: a server JSON.
- Territories pay cosmetics; the season pays a title and a standard pattern.
- No content is ever gated behind the front. It is a scoreboard with a face and
  a reason to check in.

### 10.3 Acceptance

- After a solo Skirmish win the camp says the front moved and names the
  territory.
- The numbers shown are real counts from real matches, never simulated.

## 11. S7: Rivals

### 11.1 What it is

One named Crown captain per player who counters their last five banners, taunts
them in the feed, and can be beaten for a bounty.

### 11.2 Rules

- `rivals` sheet: id, name, house, doctrine, taunt set, bounty.
- He appears every fourth Skirmish, or after three straight wins, with a banner
  built from `botBanners` and the doctrine against the player's recent Houses. No
  new AI: the Phase 2 AI spec's General and doctrines do the work.
- A first win pays a bounty and a cosmetic; he withdraws and a new rival rises
  after five matches. A loss pays him a front point in 0.9.
- In the campaign the named cast already carries this role; rivals are Skirmish
  only.

### 11.3 Acceptance

- The player can describe their rival's banner from memory.

## 12. S8: The Chronicle and Photo Mode

### 12.1 What it is

The end-of-match report becomes a card a player can post, and photo mode turns
the title-demo staging harness into a player feature.

### 12.2 Rules

- The chronicle card is 1080 by 1080 px, drawn in the UI kit: banner colour,
  mode, map, condition, score, MVP (prefer a sergeant), the standard's fate, one
  line of copy from a sheet. Saved to `screenshots/`; the report screen offers
  the path.
- Photo mode (F9 by default): HUD hidden, free camera, pose keys for the
  commander, up to four posed troops, shutter. Reuses the TitleDemos camera and
  Phase 4's `actor`/`pose` code. Not available in a ranked or public match if one
  ever exists; unlimited in solo.

### 12.3 Acceptance

- One chronicle card is good enough to post without editing.
- Photo mode saves a usable image in under ten seconds from the keypress.

## 13. S9: The camp that grows, and the immersion pass

### 13.1 What it is

The hub reflects the war, and the battle feels like a battle: sound, camera and
life.

### 13.2 Rules

- **Camp stages** (from 0.9, because they follow chapters 2 to 5): the camp gains
  one authored structure set per chapter completed (a companion's tent, a trophy
  row, a second fire, the graveyard's stones, the front's banner). One set per
  stage in `campStages`, placed by Phase 3's `Structures` and swapped when the
  chapter count changes.
- **Audio layers** (0.6): war ambience scaled by living troop count (three
  layers, crossfaded over 2 s), rain and storm loops tied to the condition, a low
  drum under a charge that fades with the charge's end. All from `sounds.json`
  entries the build already owns or adds; no third-party audio.
- **Camera beats** (0.6): 0.12 s hit-stop on a commander kill, 0.4 s time
  dilation on a standard falling, a commander death camera that holds 1.5 s and
  returns. All client-only, skippable with a setting.
- **Life** (0.6): camp NPCs walk between stations, banners move with wind,
  fireflies at night, one camp dog. Server entities with the camp tag, discarded
  on rebuild like every other camp entity.

### 13.3 Acceptance

- A tester can tell a large battle from a small one with their eyes closed.
- The camp reads as inhabited in a still screenshot.
- Every beat can be turned off.

## 14. S10: The First Hour

### 14.1 What it is

A scripted six-step chain that guarantees a new player meets the game's best
moments inside 45 minutes.

### 14.2 Rules

Steps, in order, each firing once: win battle 1; recruit once; have a sergeant
named in the feed; evolve once; win a Skirmish in a condition; reach the chapter
1 cliffhanger. The final step pays one cosmetic. The camp guide's return line
names what changed since the last visit (a promotion, the front, a rival's
taunt). A local funnel log is written to `%LOCALAPPDATA%\gravesandglory\`;
nothing leaves the machine.

### 14.3 Acceptance

- A fresh profile reaches all six steps inside 45 minutes.
- The funnel log shows which step a player stopped on.

## 15. Data model

### 15.1 `conditions` (new, 0.6)

| Column | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| id | S | `calm`, `rain`, `fog`, `night`, `blizzard`, `gale` |
| name | S | shown on the field card |
| glyph | S | `gravesandglory:textures/gui/condition/<id>.png`, 16 px |
| blurb | S | one line for the picker |
| weight | I | the `Any` roll; sums to 100 |
| night | B | sets the world time to 18000 at setup |
| weather | S | `clear` or `rain` |
| fog | D | 0.0 to 1.0, client fog density |
| shotMult | D | multiplies every ranged hit |
| moveMult | D | multiplies troop walk speed |
| routDelta | D | added to the rout share (negative steadies) |
| riseBonus | I | points added to the Rise Again chance |
| cover | B | every attacker counts as under cover (ambush) |
| feed | S | the line the feed shows at the start |

Draft rows: calm 45/100, nothing; rain 15, weather rain, shot 0.85, move 0.97;
fog 10, fog 0.6, shot 0.80, move 0.97, cover true; night 15, night true, shot
0.90, riseBonus 5; blizzard 10, fog 0.5, shot 0.75, move 0.85; gale 5, shot 0.70,
move 1.0, routDelta 0.02.

`maps` gains `conditions` (a comma list of allowed ids; every map allows all
six at 0.6). `campaignBattles` gains `condition` (one id; `march-1` calm,
`march-2` night, `march-3` calm, `march-4` fog, `march-5` blizzard, `march-6`
night, matching the scenes). `botCommand` gains `fieldPick` (0.6: all false;
0.9 lets Hard prefer its House's condition).

### 15.2 `standards` (new, 0.6)

| Column | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| id | S | `dom_standard` |
| mode | S | a `modes` id |
| hp | I | 80 |
| auraRadius | D | 20.0 |
| routDelta | D | -0.05 inside the aura |
| regroupBonusSeconds | I | 5 |
| arrowMult | D | 0.25 |
| siegeMult | D | 4.0 |
| scoreValue | I | 40 |
| routPenalty | D | 0.10 for 60 s after a break |
| dropReturnSeconds | I | 120 |
| carrierSpeed | D | 0.9 |

### 15.3 `sergeants`, `barrowSites` (new, 0.7)

`sergeants`: `id, name, byname, deathRule, battles, title, line` (24 rows; the
copy owns names, bynames and lines). `barrowSites`: `id, map, x, z, name` (five
rows; coordinates are chosen at implementation, proven walkable and 60 to 90
blocks from the centre).

### 15.4 Profile schema 4 (0.7)

Additive over schema 3, forward tolerant: `sergeants` (up to 3 records),
`roll` (list), `barrow` (map to troop counts), `barrowBuried`, `oaths`, `feats`,
`houseXp`, `front`, `rival`, `campStage`, `chronicleSeed`. `sanitize` repairs
nulls, drops ids no sheet knows, and leaves unknown-but-valid fields alone.
`ProfileMigration` adds `v3toV4`: an empty roster of sergeants, the roll empty,
the barrow empty; nothing is invented for a player who never had them.

### 15.5 Sheets for 0.8 and 0.9

`oaths`, `feats`, `houseChapters`, `warfront`, `rivals`, `chronicle` (copy),
`campStages`. Columns are named in their release's spec; 0.6 does not add them.

### 15.6 Packets

- 0.6: the merged base's STATE (currently designed as `state_v9`) becomes
  `state_v10`: `String condition`, then per team `float standardHp`, `int
  standardState` (0 up, 1 down, 2 carried, 3 returning), `int carrierEntityId`,
  inserted immediately before the JSON snapshot string so the snapshot stays
  last. The client reads them in the same place.
- 0.7: `state_v11` adds nothing binary; sergeant data rides in the snapshot JSON
  (`sergeants`, `roll` sizes). The barrow's risen are ordinary entities.
- Any later layout change bumps the channel id again.

## 16. Harness and gates

Standalone (seconds, no game):
- `ConditionCheck`: every multiplier, the weighted pick over 10,000 rolls within
  2 per cent, the blizzard and gale order of operations, the night rise bonus
  cap.
- `StandardCheck`: aura selection, damage multipliers by kind, score and return
  arithmetic, the teardown invariants.
- `SergeantCheck` (0.7): promotion order, succession, milestones, mode rules.
- `BarrowCheck` (0.7): sizing, cap, selection, burial cost and shards.
- `OathCheck`, `FrontCheck` (0.9): draws, progress, thresholds.

In-game (`Phase6Checks`, `p6-*` ids): the cond, std, cam, audio and camp suites
named in the plan. Every suite ends with `pass p6-<suite>-suite`. Every named
screenshot exists and shows its claim.

Art gate: six condition seals, the standard banner and its two states, the
sergeant chevron, the chronicle template and the front map are graded 1 to 10
against `work/ui-concepts/GRADING-RUBRIC.md`; below 9 is a fix round.

Kort's gates: 0.6 three matches in different conditions and one standard steal;
0.7 a playtest where he names his sergeants; 0.8 a 3v3 with friends; 0.9 a week
of play; 1.0 a stranger's first hour.

## 17. Metrics and gates

Local funnel log only; no external telemetry without Kort's word.

- Session: median 45 minutes or more, three matches or more.
- Draft retention targets: D1 35 per cent, D7 15, D30 6.
- Funnel: first win 12 minutes or less; first cosmetic 30 minutes or less; first
  sergeant named 20 minutes or less; chapter 1 finished 3 hours or less; chapter
  2 started within two days of chapter 1.
- Attachment: at least half of players who finish chapter 1 can name a sergeant.

## 18. Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
