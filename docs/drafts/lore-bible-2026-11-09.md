## 1. The saga in one paragraph

The land, which humans call Wakeland, is the bodies of earthen giants. Ages ago they grew tired of being alive, built a spire, Merudaal, "the Last Breath", that breathed their glimpur out for good, and crumbled into mountains, plains and sea floor. The meru leaked out as glimpur, and it is the magic in every living thing. Now the spire has woken. The giants are rising, calm and courteous, and they want every drop of glimpur destroyed so it can never be used for harm. They are right that it has been, above all by humans. They are monstrous because the cure is the end of four Houses, and they threw the glimpur away themselves. Act I (seasons 1 to 5; chapter 1 ships in 1.0) is five Houses fighting over old wrongs while the land quietly shows signs of life. The saga ends when the giants choose to live and share what they threw away.

## 3. Timeline

**[call]** Dates are for consistency, not for display.

- Ages ago: the giants build Merudaal, breathe out their glimpur, and crumble. Glimpur spreads into everything.
- About 900 years ago: Ironclad's ancestors land from Demacrus; the Concord's realms follow and build Highheart on Orruvan's chest, above Merudaal.
- The following centuries: the Gilded hunt the Wildmoot; mages bombard the sea; the Ironclad tap and graft redstone.
- About 150 years ago: in Tengervald, the dying realm the Concord looked away from, Halvard learns to bind the dead with glimpur. The binding is the first great misuse and the one that trips the spire.
- This year: the spire pulls glimpur home, the binding frays, the dead think again and rebel. 1.0 begins.

## 4. The giants

**What they are.** Regional elders, each a different part of the land. Five lie under Wakeland; more lie beyond the sea. They share one will, led by the first to wake, until the Houses break it.

| Elder | Body | Name | Reading | House myth |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Leader | under Highheart | Orruvan | orru-van, "counted enough": it counted its years and said enough | Gilded scholars dismiss it as old stone |
| Mountains | under Thruggsen | Kharuun | khar-uun, "fire heart" | Ironclad: the Makers |
| Mountains | under Tengervald | Ysmorren | ys-morren, "quiet under snow" | Graveborn: the Rest |
| Plains | the grasslands | Ommeru | om-meru, "wide breath": the plains wind | Wildmoot: the Great Herd Beneath |
| Sea | the sea floor | Thalluun | thal-luun, "deep mother" | Tidecallers: the Deep Mother (their myth kept the meaning) |

The names are confirmed in `docs/lore/names.md` (Kort may veto any one). Giant names are built from a few roots (orru, van, meru, om, daal, khar, uun, ys, morren, thal, luun), so a player who learns them can read every giant name. Ommeru and Merudaal share the root meru on purpose. Nobody speaks these names in Act I.

**Orruvan.** The most tired of the giants. It woke because the spire pulled it back, not because it wants to be alive. The cure is its grief, not its crusade. Convincing it that life is worth living is the saga's emotional ending.

**Voice.** Calm, courteous, inevitable, never raised. Violet-white glow in the glimpur they pull. They offer terms; they never threaten.

**What they want and why it frightens.** They want all glimpur destroyed so it cannot be used for harm. They are right about the harm. They are wrong in three ways the Houses can say aloud:
- The cure ends four Houses, and the giants count them as residue, not people.
- Every House except the Gilded is, in the giants' eyes, an outcome of glimpur used for evil, so no terms are offered to them.
- The giants threw the glimpur away themselves, out of weariness, and now claim to guard it.

**What angers them most.** The Skeleton King's binding. The dead are meant to rest.

**Why the spire woke.** The binding crossed a threshold. The humans caused their own reckoning.

**Machine and crystal.** Their civilization was clockwork and glimpur-crystal engines. Redstone is a fragment of it. This makes the Ironclad the giants' nearest kin, which the giants refuse to acknowledge.

## 5. The five Houses

Each House is both wronged and wrongdoer. The grievance ring runs: Gilded hunted Wildmoot; Wildmoot raided Ironclad; Ironclad poisoned the Tidecallers' rivers; Tidecallers drowned Gilded coast towns; the Graveborn are distrusted by all.

### Gilded Order (human, Lord Marshal Aldric)

Humans from Demacrus who founded the realms of the Concord. Highheart stands on Orruvan's chest. They are sworn to the Charter, which protects every person under it and was never meant to include animals or the sea. They hunted the Wildmoot for centuries, and their mages bombarded the sea. The Skeleton King was one of their kings, so they are complicit in the first great misuse.

- Wronged by: Tidecallers, who drowned their coast towns.
- Did wrong to: Wildmoot.
- Hint in 1.0 (chapter 1): spells sputter; a quarry uncovers a carved stone shape.
- Act II: offered the only terms. Aldric is the likeliest to accept; his oath is both his reason to refuse and his reason to accept.
- Aesthetic: Oathbound; steel, white, gold, banners of the Charter.

### Wildmoot (animals, Old Horn)

Grazing animals who hid when humans began hunting and adapted over centuries into something fierce and bloodthirsty for humans. They grazed grass rooted in giant flesh and rich in glimpur, which is why centuries of hunting made them cunning, not extinct **[call]**.

- Wronged by: Gilded.
- Did wrong to: Ironclad, whose mining caravans they raided.
- Hint (chapter 2, season 2): herds flee uphill; the grass withers.
- Tone: loud, feral grief and vengeance. No violet. The calm dread belongs to the giants.
- Aesthetic: Thornhide; boar-kin, wolf, spider, antler. Keep as shipped.

### Ironclad (forge people, Brassjaw)

A clan of expert geologists from Demacrus, among the first humans to land. They settled Thruggsen and used all of it. They tapped redstone veins, which are glimpur crystallised in giant bone, and grafted them into themselves and their mechs until their descendants became half machine.

- Wronged by: Wildmoot.
- Did wrong to: Tidecallers, whose rivers their smelters poisoned.
- Hint (chapter 3, season 3): redstone veins pulse toward the mountains; the forges lose heat.
- Act II: divided. Some hope the giants will treat them as kin; the giants refuse.
- Aesthetic: Forgewall; soot iron and brass shift toward redstone-red glow and visible machine **[call: palette pass needed]**.

### Tidecallers (sea and water people, Admiral Vael)

Marine life, once only fish, sharks and shrimp. Mages with no safe place to experiment bombarded the ocean with fire, lightning and poison. The sea's glimpur remembered the human casters and remade the marine life in their image **[call]**. It was painful, and it made them furious and ready to avenge their water.

- Wronged by: Ironclad.
- Did wrong to: Gilded, whose coast towns they drowned.
- Hint (chapter 4, season 4): the tide pulls inland; their glow fades.
- Aesthetic: Tidewrought; unchanged beyond the origin.

### Graveborn (rebels from the king's army, the Hollow King)

The bound dead of the Skeleton King's army who broke the binding this year. The spire's first pulse began pulling glimpur home, which frayed the binding, and the dead woke up. They do not know why they can think again. The other Houses distrust them because they were once part of the king's army and because of what they did while bound.

- Wronged by: the Skeleton King and the suspicion of everyone.
- Did wrong to: Wildmoot and Tidecallers, while bound: razing Wildmoot dens and sinking Tidecaller ships **[call: victims are mine]**.
- Hint (chapter 5, season 5): the binding frays; the rebellion itself is the hint.
- Aesthetic: Barrow Warden; bone and rusted iron, with the broken binding shown as cut cords or cracked sigils.

## 6. The Skeleton King

Halvard of Tengervald, called the Holdfast in life: Halvard is an old mountain name meaning "guardian of the rock", and his people called him the Holdfast because he would not let a pass fall or a village go. Tengervald was one of the Concord's realms. When the Concord struck it from its relief list he bound the dead with glimpur to defend it and held on past death, his people's and his own, long past the point of decency. The Charter's failure made him. He is tragic as well as guilty. A holdfast is also the thing that will not let go, which is what the binding is.

- He leads the Bound Host, a hostile NPC army, not playable. In 1.0 only his bound dead appear (chapter 1, battle 2). The Bound Host arrives as an NPC enemy faction in season 5, where chapter 5's finale is the one fight every House can join.
- The army breaks in the finale; he survives and withdraws **[call]**.
- Act II: he keeps hunting the Houses, so they face two enemies at once.
- Act III: the giants execute him before the Houses as an example of what harm earns. It should land as deserved and sad.
- Name use: Act I player text calls him the Skeleton King or leaves him unnamed. **[call]** "Halvard" is first spoken in Act II or III.

## 8. The long arc

| Act | Job |
|---|---|
| I (seasons 1 to 5) | Five House chapters. Hints only. The ring stays open. |
| II | Orruvan wakes under Highheart. The giants arrive as a non-playable enemy. The Houses split: Gilded offered terms; Ironclad refused kinship; Wildmoot, Tidecallers and Graveborn fight with no terms. The Skeleton King hunts everyone. |
| III | The remaining elders wake. The giants execute the Skeleton King. Their will fractures as the Houses prove life has worth. Orruvan chooses to live. The giants become a playable House. |
| After | New arcs on a settled world: Demacrus and other giant bodies. |

**[call]** How the Houses prove life has worth is left to Act III's writer.

**Campaign pattern in Act I [call].** Each chapter has six battles (fixed difficulty, order lock). Battles 1 to 3 fight the House that wronged the chapter's House. Battles 4 and 5 are the chapter's own wrong against the next House. Battle 6 faces that next House's Legendary (Old Horn, Brassjaw, Vael, Aldric), who withdraws alive and leads the next chapter; its bonus is never "kill the Legendary", since the engine's `kill_unit` bonus only pays on a kill. Chapter 5 breaks the pattern: its enemy is the Bound Host and its allies are the two Houses the Graveborn wronged. Every battle names the tactic it teaches or tests. Outlines: `chapter-1-outline-2026-11-09.md` to `chapter-5-outline-2026-11-09.md`; chapter 1's approved battles are `docs/lore/chapter-1-battles.md`.

## 9. Acts and seasons

Server seasons are one month long. Chapter N arrives in season N; chapter 1 ships in 1.0. The design's three story "seasons" are now acts (`docs/lore/season-framework.md`):

| Act | Was called | Seasons | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| I. The Ring | season 1 | 1 to 5 | One House chapter per season. Giants only hinted. Ends with the spire's light. |
| II. The Waking | season 2 | 6 to 8 | Orruvan wakes under Highheart. Giants are a non-playable enemy from season 6. The Houses split; the Gilded are offered terms. |
| III. The Choice | season 3 | 9 to 11 | The other elders wake, execute the Skeleton King, and their will breaks. Orruvan chooses to live. Giants become a playable House. |
| After | after | 12 on | New arcs: Demacrus, new peoples, other giant bodies. |

Each season ships its chapter, at least one unit and one giant thread step. New maps come only where the story needs a place the shipped five cannot be: the Drowned Coast (season 4), Tengervald (season 5), Highheart (season 6).

## 11. Voice and writing rules

- Text rules carry over from the old spec: ASCII, no dashes as punctuation, no exclamation marks, short lines.
- Each House has one voice: Gilded, formal and dutiful; Wildmoot, loud and grieving; Ironclad, blunt and practical; Tidecallers, fluid and furious; Graveborn, dry and tired.
- The giants speak last, never raise their voice, and do not threaten. Their courtesy is the horror.
- The silent commander has no lines. The player speaks through the House and its Legendary.
- The old cast (Danta, Maud, Wren, Dunmar, Ashka, Edric Vane, Pell, Tomas) is retired with no cameos. The Legendaries are the whole named cast; supporting figures are titled, not named.
- Chapter 1's player text uses only Tengervald, the Charter and Aldric; Wakeland, Highheart and the Concord may appear in a later story layer.
