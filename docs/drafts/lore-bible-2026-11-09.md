If this bible and the design disagree, the design wins and this file is wrong. Calls I made alone are marked **[call]**; everything else is Kort's decision. Nothing here changes mechanics.

## 1. The saga in one paragraph

The land, which humans call Eldmark, is the bodies of earthen giants. Ages ago they grew bored of being alive, built a spire that drained their mana, and crumbled into mountains, plains and sea floor. The mana leaked out as glimpur, and it is the magic in every living thing. Now the spire has woken. The giants are rising, calm and courteous, and they want every drop of glimpur destroyed so it can never be used for harm. They are right that it has been, above all by humans. They are monstrous because the cure is the end of four Houses, and they threw the mana away themselves. Season 1 (1.0.0) is five Houses fighting over old wrongs while the land quietly shows signs of life. The saga ends when the giants choose to live and share what they threw away.

## 3. Timeline

**[call]** Dates are for consistency, not for display.

- Ages ago: the giants build the spire, drain their mana, and crumble. Glimpur spreads into everything.
- About 900 years ago: Ironclad's ancestors land from Demacrus; the confederacy's realms follow and build their capital on the leader elder's chest, above the spire.
- The following centuries: the Gilded hunt the Wildmoot; mages bombard the sea; the Ironclad tap and graft redstone.
- About 150 years ago: in Tengervald, the dying realm the confederacy looked away from, a king learns to bind the dead with glimpur. The binding is the first great misuse and the one that trips the spire.
- This year: the spire pulls glimpur home, the binding frays, the dead think again and rebel. 1.0.0 begins.

## 4. The giants

**What they are.** Regional elders, each a different part of the land. Five lie on Eldmark; more lie beyond the sea. They share one will, led by the first to wake, until the Houses break it.

| Elder | Body | Name | Translation | House myth |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Leader | under the Gilded capital | Orruvan | Counted Enough | Gilded scholars dismiss it as old stone |
| Mountains | under Thruggsen | Kharuun | Furnace Heart | Forge: the Makers |
| Mountains | under Tengervald | Ysmorren | Quiet Under Snow | Graveborn: the Rest |
| Plains | the grasslands | Ommeru | Wide Breath | Wildmoot: the Great Herd Beneath |
| Sea | the sea floor | Thalluun | Deep Mother | Tidecallers: the Deep Mother |

The names and translations are my placeholders **[call]**. Nobody speaks them in season 1.

**Orruvan.** The most tired of the giants. It woke because the spire pulled it back, not because it wants to be alive. The cure is its grief, not its crusade. Convincing it that life is worth living is the saga's emotional ending.

**Voice.** Calm, courteous, inevitable, never raised. Violet-white glow in the mana they pull. They offer terms; they never threaten.

**What they want and why it frightens.** They want all glimpur destroyed so it cannot be used for harm. They are right about the harm. They are wrong in three ways the Houses can say aloud:
- The cure ends four Houses, and the giants count them as residue, not people.
- Every House except the Gilded is, in the giants' eyes, an outcome of glimpur used for evil, so no terms are offered to them.
- The giants threw the mana away themselves, out of boredom, and now claim to guard it.

**What angers them most.** The Skeleton King's binding. The dead are meant to rest.

**Why the spire woke.** The binding crossed a threshold. The humans caused their own reckoning.

**Machine and crystal.** Their civilization was clockwork and glimpur-crystal engines. Redstone is a fragment of it. This makes the Ironclad the giants' nearest kin, which the giants refuse to acknowledge.

## 5. The five Houses

Each House is both wronged and wrongdoer. The grievance ring runs: Gilded hunted Wildmoot; Wildmoot raided Ironclad; Ironclad poisoned the Tidecallers' rivers; Tidecallers drowned Gilded coast towns; the Graveborn are distrusted by all.

### Gilded Order (human, Lord Marshal Aldric)

Humans from Demacrus who founded the realms. Their capital stands on the leader elder's chest. They are sworn to the Charter, which protects every person under it and was never meant to include animals or the sea. They hunted the Wildmoot for centuries, and their mages bombarded the sea. The Skeleton King was one of their kings, so they are complicit in the first great misuse.

- Wronged by: Tidecallers, who drowned their coast towns.
- Did wrong to: Wildmoot.
- Hint in 1.0.0: spells sputter; a quarry uncovers a giant's eye.
- Season 2: offered the only terms. Aldric is the likeliest to accept; his oath is both his reason to refuse and his reason to accept.
- Aesthetic: Oathbound; steel, white, gold, banners of the Charter.

### Wildmoot (animals, Old Horn)

Grazing animals who hid when humans began hunting and adapted over centuries into something fierce and bloodthirsty for humans. They grazed grass rooted in giant flesh and rich in glimpur, which is why centuries of hunting made them cunning, not extinct **[call]**.

- Wronged by: Gilded.
- Did wrong to: Ironclad, whose mining caravans they raided.
- Hint: herds flee uphill; the grass withers.
- Tone: loud, feral grief and vengeance. No violet. The calm dread belongs to the giants.
- Aesthetic: Thornhide; boar-kin, wolf, spider, antler. Keep as shipped.

### Ironclad (forge people, Brassjaw)

A clan of expert geologists from Demacrus, among the first humans to land. They settled Thruggsen and used all of it. They tapped redstone veins, which are glimpur crystallised in giant bone, and grafted them into themselves and their mechs until their descendants became half machine.

- Wronged by: Wildmoot.
- Did wrong to: Tidecallers, whose rivers their smelters poisoned.
- Hint: redstone veins pulse toward the mountains; the forges lose heat.
- Season 2: divided. Some hope the giants will treat them as kin; the giants refuse.
- Aesthetic: Forgewall; soot iron and brass shift toward redstone-red glow and visible machine **[call: palette pass needed]**.

### Tidecallers (sea and water people, Admiral Vael)

Marine life, once only fish, sharks and shrimp. Mages with no safe place to experiment bombarded the ocean with fire, lightning and poison. The sea's glimpur remembered the human casters and remade the marine life in their image **[call]**. It was painful, and it made them furious and ready to avenge their water.

- Wronged by: Ironclad.
- Did wrong to: Gilded, whose coast towns they drowned.
- Hint: the tide pulls inland; their glow fades.
- Aesthetic: Tidewrought; unchanged beyond the origin.

### Graveborn (rebels from the king's army, the Hollow King)

The bound dead of the Skeleton King's army who broke the binding this year. The spire's first pulse began pulling glimpur home, which frayed the binding, and the dead woke up. They do not know why they can think again. The other Houses distrust them because they were once part of the king's army and because of what they did while bound.

- Wronged by: the Skeleton King and the suspicion of everyone.
- Did wrong to: Wildmoot and Tidecallers, while bound: razing Wildmoot dens and sinking Tidecaller ships **[call: victims are mine]**.
- Hint: the binding frays; the rebellion itself is the hint.
- Aesthetic: Barrow Warden; bone and rusted iron, with the broken binding shown as cut cords or cracked sigils.

## 6. The Skeleton King

A human king of Tengervald, one of the confederacy's realms. The confederacy looked away from his dying realm; he bound the dead with glimpur to defend it and kept going long past the point of decency. The Charter's failure made him. He is tragic as well as guilty.

- He leads a hostile NPC army, not playable. In 1.0.0 he is the enemy with a leader and the finale's one fight every House can join.
- The army breaks in the finale; he survives and withdraws **[call]**.
- Season 2: he keeps hunting the Houses, so they face two enemies at once.
- Season 3: the giants execute him before the Houses as an example of what harm earns. It should land as deserved and sad.
- Unnamed in this document **[call]**.

## 8. The long arc

| Season | Job |
|---|---|
| 1 (1.0.0) | Five House chapters. Hints only. The ring stays open. |
| 2 | Orruvan wakes under the capital. The giants arrive as a non-playable enemy. The Houses split: Gilded offered terms; Forge refused kinship; Wildmoot, Tidecallers and Graveborn fight with no terms. The Skeleton King hunts everyone. |
| 3 | The remaining elders wake. The giants execute the Skeleton King. Their will fractures as the Houses prove life has worth. Orruvan chooses to live. The giants become a playable House. |
| After | New arcs on a settled world: Demacrus and other giant bodies. |

**[call]** How the Houses prove life has worth is left to season 3's writer.

**Campaign pattern in 1.0.0 [call].** Each chapter has six battles (fixed difficulty, order lock). Battles 1 to 3 fight the House that wronged the chapter's House. Battles 4 and 5 are the chapter's own wrong against the next House. Battle 6 faces that next House's Legendary (Old Horn, Brassjaw, Vael, Aldric), who withdraws alive and leads the next chapter. Chapter 5 breaks the pattern: its enemy is the Skeleton King's army and its allies are the two Houses the Graveborn wronged. ## 9. Seeds planted in 1.0.0

- Giant relics on maps (statues, a stone hand, a carved eye) so the land looks like it is looking back.
- Glimpur ebbing in play: a visual and audio flicker on magic troops in later chapters, no mechanical effect **[call]**.
- Each House's folk myth, a distorted memory of an elder.
- Archive items that show the Skeleton King was a Gilded realm's king.

## 10. Voice and writing rules

- Text rules carry over from the old spec: ASCII, no dashes as punctuation, no exclamation marks, short lines.
- Each House has one voice: Gilded, formal and dutiful; Wildmoot, loud and grieving; Ironclad, blunt and practical; Tidecallers, fluid and furious; Graveborn, dry and tired.
- The giants speak last, never raise their voice, and do not threaten. Their courtesy is the horror.
- The silent commander has no lines. The player speaks through the House and its Legendary.
- The old cast (Danta, Maud, Wren, Dunmar, Ashka, Edric Vane, Pell, Tomas) is retired with no cameos. The Legendaries are the whole named cast; supporting figures are titled, not named.
